Design Decode ’26 · Redesign challenge

Mountain Retreat — from loud
to lucid.

The original screen shouts everything at once: eight colours, five fonts and a box around every fact. The redesign keeps every piece of content and both actions, but gives the page one clear reading order: photo → identity → trust → facts → story → book.

1

Every element is shouting

Red-on-yellow title, five tinted boxes, and gradients that all fight for attention. There is no focal point.

Hierarchy

One focal point, then quiet

A 38 pt editorial serif title is the only loud element. Everything else uses one neutral ink scale on white paper.

Hierarchy · Typography
2

The location reads like a button

An all-caps pink pill looks tappable but does nothing. All caps also slows reading.

Affordance

Plain text plus a real Map link

The location is set in sentence case with a pin icon. The only tappable part is the blue “Map ↗” link.

Interaction logic
3

Five huge stars for one number

Five large stars take a whole row just to say 5.0, and “reviews” is underlined purple like a web link.

Efficiency

A compact trust line

★ 5.0 · 23 reviews (tappable) · Guest favourite badge. Social proof fits on one scannable line.

Clarity
4

Numbers with no meaning

“2, 1, 1” sits under icons with no labels, so users have to guess. Each tile also uses a different colour.

Accessibility

Labelled facts in one group

Each fact has an icon, a value and a label. “Sleeps 4”, which was hidden in the paragraph, becomes the first fact users see.

Clarity · Recognition
5

A wall of text, said twice

Two paragraphs repeat “cozy” and the setting. The key selling points are buried in prose.

Scannability

“Why you’ll love it” highlights

The prose becomes three icon highlights (views, hike & fish, cosy interior), followed by one short paragraph with “Read more”.

Content design
6

Contrast fails

The grey text on lavender is about 2.4:1 and the yellow stars on yellow about 1.3:1, both below WCAG AA.

Accessibility

All body text passes AA

Ink is 17.4:1, body text 9.3:1 and muted text 5.5:1. Nothing relies on colour alone, and every tap target is at least 44 pt.

WCAG 2.2 AA
7

Two competing CTAs

A neon-green button and a hot-pink price look equally important. The price has no dates or context.

User flow

One sticky action with context

The price, chosen dates and guests sit on the left of a single dark “Book now” button, which opens a booking sheet.

Flow · Fitts’ law
8

A red back button and a tiny counter

The back button uses the colour for error/destructive actions. “1/8” is the only hint that more photos exist.

Consistency

Glass controls and a visible gallery

Neutral glass back and share buttons sit on the photo. Thumbnails overlap the card with a “+4” tile, and Save is a floating heart.

Design system · Rationale

One screen, one quiet system.

Every colour, size and radius on the screen comes from a small set of tokens. That keeps the page consistent, keeps it accessible by default, and makes it easy to extend to the booking flow that “Book now” opens.

System rules

  • Paper card · 80% heightThe card is inset 10 pt with a 32 pt radius and one shadow. The photo stays visible above it, and the thumbnails break out of its edge.
  • 4-pt spacing gridSpacing steps are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 pt. Radii are 12 (icons), 16 (inputs), 18 (buttons), 20 (groups) and 32 (card).
  • One primary action per screenIt is always dark ink, at least 56 pt tall, and placed in the bottom thumb zone.
  • Accent means interactiveGlacier blue is used only for links and selected states, never for decoration.
